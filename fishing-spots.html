How the captain decides

Fishing grounds are selected by conditions, not by a fixed pin

Offshore fishing is dynamic. Productive water can shift as current, bait movement, wind, tide, and visibility change. A responsible charter therefore treats every named area as a route zone rather than a guaranteed spot.

01

Current and tide

Passes, wall edges, and reef corners can concentrate bait when moving water forms pressure zones and feeding lanes.

02

Underwater structure

Drop-offs, slopes, reef ledges, and deeper bottom contours determine where different fish may hold and which tackle is practical.

03

Target and technique

Trolling covers water for mobile pelagic fish, while jigging and bottom dropping work specific depths and structure.

04

Safety and comfort

Wind, swell, travel time, guest experience, and vessel limits always take priority over reaching a planned fishing ground.

Tier 1 · Premium route

Reef walls · current breaks · versatile techniques

The Middle Pass & Caubian Islands

This route explores the Caubian-side passages and structured edges of the Danajon reef system. It is the more versatile option for groups that want a mix of trolling, jigging, casting, and bottom-oriented fishing without making the deepest offshore route the sole focus.

Capacity Up to 4 anglers
Experience level Accessible to mixed groups
Fishing environment Passes, walls, reefs, drop-offs
Trip emphasis Variety and adaptable routing
01

Wall and drop-off zone

The Caubian Wall & Deep Drop-Offs

A structured edge where changing depth and moving water can create productive lanes for predators travelling along the reef boundary.

Potential daytime targets

Giant trevally, Spanish mackerel, and barracuda.

Potential nighttime targets

Bigeye trevally and barracuda around illuminated or bait-active water.

Typical techniques

Jigging along depth changes and trolling across productive edges.

02

Bottom-structure zone

The Outer Barrier Seamounts

Deeper underwater rises and outer-reef structure where precise depth control becomes more important than simply covering distance.

Potential targets

Grouper, red snapper, emperor fish, and other structure-associated species.

Typical techniques

Slow-pitch or vertical jigging presented close to the bottom contour.

What anglers should expect

More technical depth management and repeated controlled drifts.

03

Current-driven passage

The Middle Pass Drift

A passage-oriented route where the boat can work moving water, bait signs, and surface activity rather than remaining over one fixed piece of structure.

Potential targets

Schooling jacks and passing pelagic fish, including tuna when present.

Typical techniques

Light-tackle casting, shallow trolling, and drift presentations.

What anglers should expect

A mobile search pattern guided by current seams and visible bait activity.

Tier 2 · Ultra-Premium route

Longer range · deeper water · heavier tackle

The Deep Blue Danajon Banks

This extended route pushes farther toward the outer Danajon Bank and the Camotes Sea approach. It is intended for guests who understand that longer travel, deeper presentations, and a more demanding offshore environment are part of the experience.

Capacity Up to 4 anglers
Experience level Best for experienced guests
Fishing environment Outer passes, slopes, deep water
Trip emphasis Range, depth, and heavy tackle
01

Outer passage and pelagic search zone

The Northeast Pass

The Camotes Sea-facing approach provides wider water coverage and access to deeper travel lanes where mobile pelagic species may be encountered.

Potential targets

Yellowfin tuna, dogtooth tuna, Spanish mackerel, barracuda, and deep snapper.

Typical techniques

High-speed trolling, topwater casting, deep jigging, and selected drops.

What anglers should expect

Longer search runs and technique changes based on bait, birds, and sonar marks.

02

Pass and reef-transition zone

The Caubian Middle Pass

A transition area where current, reef structure, and deeper open water meet, allowing the crew to shift between casting, trolling, and jigging.

Potential daytime targets

Giant trevally, amberjack, and passing tuna when conditions align.

Potential nighttime targets

Bigeye trevally and barracuda around bait-active reef patrol areas.

Typical techniques

Jigging, casting, trolling, and drift work selected by current strength.

03

Deep bottom and slope zone

The Danajon Deep Drop

A depth-focused section intended for heavier presentations near deep slopes and bottom structure, where tackle control and boat positioning are critical.

Potential targets

Grouper, red snapper, dogtooth tuna, amberjack, and other deep structure fish.

Typical techniques

Slow-pitch jigging and heavy bottom dropping with electric-reel support when appropriate.

What anglers should expect

Heavier gear, longer retrieves, and highly controlled positioning over depth.

Responsible expectations

A route description is not a catch guarantee

Target species describe the type of fish associated with the planned environment and techniques. Actual encounters depend on season, water conditions, fish movement, regulations, and natural variability.

The Danajon Bank is a rare and ecologically important double-barrier reef system. Guests should follow the captain’s instructions, respect protected areas, and use responsible handling and release practices whenever required.
